Design

Built to be
forgotten.

The best thing a recorder can do during a consultation is disappear. One continuous shell, one control, and a screen that answers the only question worth asking mid-take: is it still running?

Single-piece clinical shell

Light in the hand.
Steady on the desk.

It sits between clinician and patient without becoming the third thing in the room. The dial is textured deeply enough to find without looking, which matters when the alternative is breaking eye contact to check a screen.

16 kHz Enough for every measure on this page and for the speech band that carries them. The rate is pinned rather than negotiated: the FFT axis, the WAV header and every duration in the app assume it.
01

Captured off the main thread.

Recording runs in an audio worklet, so drawing a waveform can never cost you a block of audio. It used to.

02

Measured on the spot.

F0, harmonics-to-noise, jitter and shimmer are extracted the moment a take is saved — not that evening, not next week.

03

Checked before it counts.

Level, clipping and duration are reported against the task's own thresholds, so a take that will not survive analysis is caught immediately.

Privacy, stated plainly

It never leaves
this browser.

There is no backend. Patients, sessions and audio live in the browser's own storage, on one machine — which is also why a colleague on another computer cannot see them, and why clearing site data deletes the lot.

That makes this research use only. No real patient data may enter it as it stands: there is no audit log, no encryption at rest, no authentication, and no business associate agreement. The Epic connection runs against Epic's public sandbox.

The full list of what this build cannot do is kept in the open, in LIMITATIONS.md, and inside the app itself.
